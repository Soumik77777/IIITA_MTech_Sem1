HYPOTHESIS 1
Varying the trimming percentage in K-means least trimmed square (LTS) algorithms directly alters cluster separation boundaries and silhouette scores, with moderate trimming thresholds (e.g., 30%) yielding optimal cluster separation for highly skewed real-world datasets.

Rationale: Ghayatrie and Fitrianah (2026) demonstrated that applying K-means LTS with a 30% trimming percentage significantly improved silhouette scores compared to standard K-means when handling heavy-tailed academic alumni records.
Testable prediction: Increasing the trimming rate beyond the optimal contamination proportion will cause a degradation in silhouette scores due to the inadvertent removal of valid inlier data points near cluster boundaries.
Caveats: The optimal trimming percentage is data-dependent and requires prior estimation of the true noise or outlier ratio.

HYPOTHESIS 2
Integrating nonparametric pseudo-isolation score thresholding into flexible Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) or subspace clustering algorithms preserves probabilistic false-alarm rate control while successfully accommodating non-spherical geometries.

Rationale: Iannaccio and Vichi (2026) established a nonparametric pseudo-isolation framework with formal Cantelli-based probability bounds for spherical K-means, but noted limitations when applied to complex non-spherical clusters.
Testable prediction: Extending pseudo-isolation scoring to covariance-adaptive GMMs will achieve an F1-score greater than 0.95 in detecting outliers across highly elliptical synthetic benchmark distributions where standard trimmed K-means fails.
Caveats: Estimating full covariance matrices increases parameter complexity and risks overfitting on small sample sizes.
References: P015 (2609.08494v1.pdf)
