- H001: Under radially symmetric probability measures such as the uniform distribution over a disk, the standard k-means trajectory cannot exhibit indefinite slow oscillation of cluster means; rather, the asymptotic paths are strictly bounded by a Lyapunov-type potential decrease that forces finite stopping times or convergence to fixed points.
Rationale: Theorem 1 from MacQueen (1967) ensures almost sure convergence of the sum of random variables, but leaves open whether two means can oscillate indefinitely around the center. Applying stricter martingale tail bounds and potential energy strict monotonicity rules can theoretically eliminate this pathological trajectory.
Testable prediction: For any absolutely continuous probability measure with radial symmetry on a compact convex set in E^N, the sequence of cluster means generated by standard k-means visits any neighborhood of the symmetric center at most a finite number of times with probability one.

- H002: The breakdown point of the alpha-trimmed k-mean is strictly bounded from above by the infimum of the probability masses assigned to the individual components of the mixture distribution, and misspecification of the cluster count k forces the breakdown threshold to degrade continuously toward zero.
Rationale: Cuesta-Albertos et al. (1997) demonstrated that the breakdown point is at most min(alpha, q_1, ..., q_k) independently of the penalty function Phi. A deeper probabilistic formulation under varying tail conditions will prove that excess cluster centers absorb contaminated mass in a predictable asymptotic proportion.
Testable prediction: As the number of specified clusters k exceeds the true number of underlying mixture components, the exact breakdown point collapses to the minimum probability mass of the finest true sub-component present in the measure space.

- H003: Deterministic multi-start gradient descent or convex relaxation methods can achieve comparable objective values to simulated annealing on non-convex trimmed k-means problems while significantly reducing computational runtime.
Rationale: Trimmed k-means objectives are non-convex, leading current implementations to rely heavily on simulated annealing. While thorough, simulated annealing suffers from high computational overhead and lack of deterministic guarantees. Constructing localized convex relaxations or tightly bounded multi-start initializations can bypass stochastic search inefficiencies.
Testable prediction: A deterministic multi-start gradient descent algorithm combined with radius-constrained ball assignment will achieve objective function values within 1 percent of simulated annealing while executing at least 5 times faster on synthetic 2D Gaussian mixture datasets.
